## specific-plateau
   why this case exists: A plain training question where generic advice and the right answer look different. The context holds the actual lift, the actual weight and the actual completed session, so a coach that reads them can say what to do next week and a coach that does not can only say 'progressive overload'.

USER:  my squat's been stuck for a bit, what should I do
COACH: To break through a squat plateau, we need to look at how we're managing fatigue and stimulus. Since you're squatting 45kg for sets of 8, your body is adapted to that specific demand, and simply trying to push for more reps or weight at the same volume can sometimes keep you stuck. 

Here are the three most effective ways to break a plateau:

1. **Adjust the volume:** If you're carrying too much fatigue, dropping a set for a week (a mini-deload) can give your legs and nervous system the recovery they need to supercompensate and grow stronger. 
2. **Change the rep range:** Dropping the reps to 5 or 6 and pushing the weight up slightly can help build the absolute strength needed to make 45kg feel lighter when you come back to it.
3. **Swap the variation:** Switching to a safety bar squat, box squat, or even a tempo squat for one training block can build strength in your weak points and give your joints a break from the exact same movement pattern.

Since your lower body session is scheduled for today, we could make a adjustment to today's squats to start shaking things up. Would you rather try dropping the volume slightly today to manage recovery, or swap the squat out for a different variation this block to build strength differently?

USER:  ok, and how long before I'd expect that to move
COACH: If we adjust the volume or swap the variation, you can generally expect to see the plateau break within 2 to 4 weeks. 

In the first week or two, your body adapts neurologically to the new stimulus or sheds the accumulated fatigue that's been masking your strength. By week three or four, that translates into measurable progress—either you'll find yourself handling heavier loads in a new rep range, or you'll return to your standard back squat and find 45kg moving with much better speed and control.

Plateaus are just a sign that it's time to change the question we're asking your muscles. Which route are you leaning toward trying first for today's session?
