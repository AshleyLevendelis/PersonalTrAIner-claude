## SLICE 1 — H6: "2 eggs" was two grams of egg

### What was wrong (re-measured before fixing, not taken from the report)

A new gate (`test:meal-amounts`) was written first and run against the untouched tree: **182 of 234
checks failed**, and the two cards from the test log came back to the kilocalorie —
`2 scrambled eggs on 2 slices of buttered toast — 266 kcal (P 6.8g)` and
`chicken caesar wrap and a large latte — 187 kcal (P 5.1g)`. The tracer's account held:

1. The amount reader ended "unknown unit, treat the number as grams". Two eggs sent with the unit
   `egg`, `eggs`, `piece`, `each`, `item` or nothing were **2 g of egg**; a "large" latte was 1 g of
   milk; `slices` in the plural was 2 g of bread; coverage stayed 100% so nothing was flagged.
2. The coach ran a hand-copied food database that had drifted both ways: 324 foods against the
   app's 333, without the 1 Sep count fix, the accent rule or the `-oes` plural rule; and with a
   9 Sep fix (stored plurals) the app never got. Same words: 266 kcal from the coach, 417 on screen.
3. The gate that was meant to hold the two copies together (`test:food-db-parity`) compared allergen
   tags only, one way, and said so in its own header.

**One thing the report did not have:** the app's own copy had the same hole for every unit except
`whole` ("1 large latte", "2 slices", unit `eggs` were all the bare number there too), and the
custom-meal prompt's own example — "3 eggs, 150g greek yoghurt, a banana" — costed **the banana at
one gram** (319 kcal instead of 423), because "a banana" has no digit.

### What changed

- **Amounts.** `unitToGrams` returns grams **or null** (`number | null`, so every caller has to say
  what it does with "unknown"). Units are normalised (`slices of` -> slice, `eggs` -> a count, `tin`
  -> can, `tablespoons` -> tbsp, kg / oz / lb / l). A count uses the food's piece weight; the food's
  own name is accepted as a count unit (`{egg, 2, "eggs"}`); a unit named in the line itself wins
  ("2 garlic cloves", "1 salmon fillet", "half avocado"); a size the food does not list is scaled
  from its medium (small 0.85, large 1.15, extra large 1.3 — the USDA's own spread for bananas,
  apples and eggs); anything else — a bowl, a serving, a "large" of something with no piece — is
  unknown. There is no fall-through.
- **Coverage.** A line whose amount is unknown is listed in `unmatched`, named separately in a new
  `amountUnknown`, adds nothing to the calories, and costs coverage **by line** (a wrap and an
  unreadable latte is 50%, where a 1 g latte read 98%). An unknown FOOD with a known weight is still
  by weight, as before.
- **Piece weights for 70 everyday foods** (10 had one), each with its source in a comment: USDA
  FoodData Central portion weights, the UK FSA's "Food Portion Sizes", or the named supermarket
  pack. Crispbread 10 g a slice (this is the "rye crispbread ~5g" in M23), bread, wraps, fillets
  (cooked weight — the table is cooked-basis), a can of tuna 102 g drained, bacon rashers,
  sausages, whole fruit and veg, a glass of milk, handfuls of nuts.
- **One food database.** `supabase/functions/_shared/food-db.ts` is now GENERATED from
  `src/lib/food-db.ts` (`npm run sync:food-db`), byte for byte under a "do not edit" header. Merged
  both ways first: the app's file gained the stored-plural keys, the coach gained the 9 foods, the
  count fix, the accent and plural rules. `test:food-db-parity` was rewritten: the generated file is
  fresh; the source can run on Deno (no imports, nothing Node-only); same foods and identical facts
  about each; the same phrase resolves to the same food and the same meal comes to the same numbers
  in both, over a list of every phrase that has gone wrong here. The deploy script refuses a stale
  copy before it asks for the production phrase.
- **A stop before the card** (`doubtAboutLoggedMeal`, shared by the coach's handler and the card's
  verifier): an amount that could not be read, a counted food that came to under 5 g when one of
  them weighs more, or a breakfast / lunch / dinner under 100 kcal gets ONE short question in the
  app's own words — "How big was the whole milk latte? A rough weight in grams, or ml for a drink,
  is enough." — and no card. The sentence is server-authored, so the model cannot turn it back into
  a number. Snacks have no floor (a coffee is a snack).
- **The card.** "Not counted: latte." is now on the card as its warning line (`payload.unmatched`
  was documented "shown on the card" and rendered nowhere). Assumptions print once: "Assumed: large
  eggs; white toast; 10 g butter." — the word is the card's, stripped from each note, so "Assuming
  assumed" cannot recur; the coach's question reply uses the same function.
- **The prompt.** "Scale portions to the meal slot budget above" is gone from the logging rule and
  replaced with "what they ate is a fact, never something to fit to a meal's budget". The tool's
  example assumption no longer starts with "assumed".
- **Siblings fixed with it:** the shopping list (an unreadable count stays a count, marked "Rough
  estimate", never added as grams; five crispbreads are 50 g); "a banana" / "an egg" / "half an
  avocado" / "two eggs" are read as amounts when the food can be weighed; "2 slices of toast" names
  the food "toast", not "of toast"; a pinch and a dash are trace measures; a custom meal refused for
  an unreadable amount says "I couldn't put a weight on hummus" instead of the false "I don't have
  hummus in my food data".

### Decisions, and whose they were

- **Generate, don't compare** (the task offered either): decided unprompted, reversible. Basis: a
  behavioural gate can only catch drift on the phrases somebody thought to list; a generated file
  cannot drift at all, and the gate still states the behavioural property.
- **The diet check was deliberately NOT given the merged lookup. Decided unprompted, reversible (one
  line in `diet-rules.ts`), and it is a deviation worth reading.** The task said to merge both
  directions and also not to change how names are matched on the allergen path. Those collide:
  measured, bringing the coach's stored-plural keys into the app's lookup changes 106 of 10,827
  corpus phrases, and 88 that were a miss now match — including dishes ("prawn cocktail" -> prawns,
  "sausage roll" -> pork sausage, "peanut sauce" -> peanuts). For costing that is the 9 Sep fix
  working. On the diet check a miss is refused for every restriction and a match is judged by one
  entry's tags, so the same change would have turned "refused" into "judged as plain prawns". So
  the costing lookup is merged (as instructed; "chocolate rice cake" now costs in both), and the
  diet check reads the names exactly as stored (`lookupIngredientAsStored`). **All 238,194 diet
  verdicts (10,827 phrases x 22 restrictions) are identical to before**, held by a gate. Cost of the
  caution, named: the diet check keeps today's known wrong reading of bare "sausage" (vegan sausage,
  passes vegetarian) and still over-refuses singulars like "1 rice cake". Both are in the plan.
- **Piece weights, the size spread, the 100 kcal floor, the 5 g tripwire**: decided as a CSCS coach
  / nutrition generalist, population level. Basis for each figure is in the code comment beside it.
  The 100 kcal floor: one boiled egg is 78 kcal, so nothing anyone calls a breakfast, lunch or
  dinner is under it; the task named the figure.
- **What the stop and the card SAY** are new app sentences. They follow the task's wording ("How big
  was it?", "Not counted: latte", "Assumed: …") and stay short; they are Ashley's to change.
- **NOT decided, left exactly as it is:** what the coach should do with bought or restaurant food it
  has no recipe for (ask / estimate and label / decline). Owner question, in the tracer's report.
- **NOT built, on instruction:** the name matcher (fix 5). Plan written instead.

### CSCS review (it changes what a logged or generated meal is costed at)

1. *Training effect, not the number:* logged intake was understated by up to 4x on the meals people
   describe most naturally (counts and sizes). A day that read "light" drives the wrong next
   decision — eat more, when the person is already at target. Costing by the piece removes that.
2. *What it takes away:* a meal that used to log instantly at a wrong number now sometimes gets one
   question first. Generated dishes with a counted line the table cannot weigh lose coverage by line
   and can be rejected where they were accepted before with ~0 kcal for that line. Counted lines
   that CAN be weighed now carry real calories, so those dishes are scaled smaller than before
   (correctly). Measured on what can be measured here: 2 of 188 library dishes changed ("Sardines on
   crispbread" 199 -> 257 kcal, "Beef noodle soup" +3), library servability identical before and
   after in every slot; of 104 recipe-style lines probed, 55 are now weighed properly, 35 unchanged,
   14 are now unknown (cinnamon stick, medium potato, chicken thighs, dates, shallots, mango, a
   sachet of honey, and foods the table lacks). **Not measured: real stored pools and live
   generation** — no database here.
3. *Fundamentals:* energy balance and protein adequacy are only as good as the intake number; this
   moves it toward the truth and marks what it cannot cost instead of guessing.
4. *A floor or ceiling quietly redefined?* **Yes, one, and it is deliberate: `coverage`.** It was
   "share of the meal's weight that resolved"; it is now that share times the share of lines whose
   amount could be read. The 80% floor is the same number and is stricter than it was. Any coverage
   figure from before 9 Oct is not comparable for meals with a counted line.
5. *Scope:* population-level costing only; no clinical nutrition.

## SLICE 2 — H10 (mechanical part) and L31: the 1 kg rule from day one

### What was wrong (re-measured first)

`test:weigh-in-targets` was written first and run on the untouched tree: **56 of 74 checks failed**.
Signing up at 82 kg and weighing in at 81.2 kg the same day moved the fixture's target from 1,946 to
1,938 kcal and protein from 164 to 162 g with no notice — the test log's 164 -> 162 exactly (the
calorie figures differ from Sam's 1,697 -> 1,689 only because the fixture's height, age and activity
are not Sam's). The tracer's account held:

- **Onboarding never recorded what set the targets.** The 1 kg rule compares the 7-day average with
  "the average that last set the target"; with nothing recorded, the first weigh-in became it.
- One row per person per day, so a same-day weigh-in REPLACES the sign-up row: the average was 81.2.
- "Today" for the 7-day window was the UTC date; the weigh-in is saved on the local date.
- The card said "Targets recalculate from your latest weigh-in · 7-day avg 62 kg" (the mean of
  however many rows exist — one); the coach's reply said the same first half.
- L31: Home subtracted the first of the last 14 weigh-in rows and called it "since week 1".

**Ashley's ruling had no gate.** No script read the anchor function or the 1 kg threshold.

### What changed

- **The first anchor is written at sign-up** (`anchorTargetsAtSignUp`: a target snapshot carrying
  the weight given) and put into the session's state. 82 -> 81.2 the same day now leaves the target
  exactly where it was and says nothing; three later days averaging 84 move it once the 7-day
  average is a kilo off, to the target for the AVERAGE, with a notice naming from and to.
- **The whole weigh-in chain is one function** (`retargetAfterWeighIn`), so the ruling is run by a
  gate instead of read off `App.tsx`.
- **One "today"** (`targetsToday`: the person's date on the app's clock) for the weigh-in, the
  7-day window and the recorded target.
- **One check for "is this believable?"** — `src/lib/weigh-in-check.ts`,
  `checkWeighIn(newKg, lastKg, daysSince)` -> ok / surprising with the signed difference. Called by
  the weigh-in card (against the weigh-in before it, which an hour later is today's own row), the
  Profile weight field (against the figure being replaced) and the coach's `log_weight` (reads the
  last weigh-in before it writes). The coach's copy is GENERATED from the app's file, like the food
  database — a second shared file through the same script, so it cannot drift.
- **Wording:** under the box, "Targets follow your 7-day average · avg of 1 weigh-in 81.2 kg" (and
  "avg of 2 weigh-ins", then "7-day avg" from three). It is the same 7-day average the targets use.
  The coach's weigh-in reply ends "Your targets follow your 7-day average."
- **L31:** Home reads "-1.0 kg since you started", measured from the starting weight (the weight
  given at sign-up; the first weigh-in ever for someone who gave none). Shown as soon as there is
  one real weigh-in; not shown while only the sign-up weight exists ("0.0 kg" on day one is noise).

### Decisions, and whose they were

- **OWNER DECISION, NOT MADE: what a surprising weigh-in does.** It is saved exactly as before. The
  verdict is held on a `data-weigh-in-check` attribute on the two screens and logged by the coach;
  it is kept out of the coach's reply and out of what the model is shown, and a gate fails if any
  other line of the handler mentions it. **So a 62 kg typo still moves the target today** — the
  difference is that it now announces itself (there is an anchor to move from). `verify:weigh-in`
  pins "nothing she can see changed" and will go red on purpose when she rules. Options and the
  tracer's recommendation (ask first, and hold the target for a second day) are in the report.
- **The thresholds inside the check: decided as a CSCS coach.** Basis: day-to-day bodyweight moves
  1-2% on water, gut content, salt and glycogen (the same figure the 1 kg rule is built on); 3% in a
  day (2.5 kg at 82 kg) is past what that explains. Plus 0.15% a day since the last weigh-in — about
  1% of bodyweight a week, the fastest change a coach would plan — so somebody back after three
  months is not questioned over 13 kg and somebody back after a year is not questioned at all (the
  tracer's risk: a real return must not be blocked). An unknown gap is read as the same day.
- **"since you started"** is the label the task named. Ashley's to change.
- **"avg of N weigh-ins" until three:** the task's wording; three is the point at which the app's
  own trend chart stops caveating a thin sample.
- Decided unprompted, reversible: the starting weight is `profile.weight_kg`, which the Profile
  screen lets her edit ("Onboarding weight") — an edit moves the baseline. Named by the tracer too.

### CSCS review (it changes when a calorie target moves)

1. *Effect, not the number:* a target that drifts on a single reading chases water weight; holding
   it until the average has really moved is what makes a deficit or surplus a plan.
2. *What it takes away:* in the first session a genuinely different first weigh-in (say 3 kg off
   the weight typed at sign-up) used to become the anchor silently; it still moves the target, and
   now says so. Nothing is slower: a move of a kilo or more acts the same day, as before.
3. *Fundamentals:* unchanged — same averaging, same threshold, same targets for a given weight.
4. *A floor or ceiling quietly redefined?* No. The 1 kg threshold is the same number meaning the
   same thing; what changed is that it now has something to compare against on day one. One wording
   change in what a figure means: the card's "avg" is now the last 7 DAYS, as the targets always
   were, where it was "the last 7 rows".
5. *Scope:* no clinical judgement; a surprising weigh-in is flagged for a person to confirm, not
   interpreted.

### Verified, and how

- `test:weigh-in-targets` (new, 76 checks) against a fake database on a clock the gate sets (the
  app's developer clock, in March, so nothing depends on the day it is run; timezone set to London
  so a day count done in local hours would show).
- `verify:weigh-in` (new, 30 checks, port 9652): real Home and real Profile at phone size. The
  label and its figure; the line under the box and that it is readable on screen; a believable
  weigh-in; 62 kg — checked, surprising by 17.4 kg, saved as before, no question, no dialog, the
  same card with different numbers; the 25-350 refusal unchanged; the Profile field asks the same
  check and saves as before. Screenshots read.
- Mutations: **43 tried, 43 caught** (35 logic, 8 in the browser).
- A driver lesson worth keeping: setting an input's value from script and calling `.blur()` left
  the Profile row unsaved (the field's blur handler never ran), so a check on "the field holds 28"
  passed over a field that saved nothing. The driver now types through the browser's own input
  pipeline and checks the stored row.

### NOT verified

- **The app's own sign-up path has not been driven end to end.** No harness page boots `App.tsx`.
  That signing up calls the anchor function, and that a saved weigh-in goes through the retarget

## SLICE 3 — L20: "on the number" and "macros off" were the header's own opinion

### What was wrong (re-measured first)

The header on Nutrition had its own rule: calories within 30 kcal AND protein within 10% AND carbs
within 10%, fat not looked at. The engine that chooses the day uses calories within 5%, protein
from 5% under to 15% over, carbs and fat within 25%. Reproduced with the header's old rule against
the engine on the untouched tree: "on the number" at 179 g of protein against 164 g, "kcal on
target, macros off" at 181 g, and "on the number" with fat 40% out; across 10,800 days the header
said "macros off" on 99 the engine had served as on target and "on the number" on 39 it called off.

### What changed

- The engine exports the verdict: `macroOnTarget(macro, actual, target)` (the day search's own
  arithmetic, moved, so a figure that sat just inside a band still does), `dayVerdict` (on target,
  or the macros that are off, worst first, each with its signed difference) and `dayVerdictLabel`.
  The day search's own check is built from the same per-macro function.
- The header prints the engine's label: "on target", or what is off — "120 over" / "200 under" for
  calories (the line already reads "target 1697 · …"), "protein 26 g over" for a macro. Calories
  are named whenever they are off; otherwise the macro furthest outside its own band. "macros off"
  is gone.
- Protein in the row beneath is lit when protein is inside the engine's band, not "at or above
  target" (which lit it at 30% over).

### Decisions

- **"on target" replaces "on the number"** — a wording change, Ashley's to rename. Basis: the
  engine's band is 5% of calories (85 kcal at 1,697), and "on the number" claims more than that.
- **Which macro is named, and calories first:** decided unprompted, reversible.
- **Not built (owner decision, the tracer's):** a way to look back at earlier days' food.
- **Not touched:** the coach prompt's own 25/35/30/10 meal budgets, which differ from the engine's
  real per-meal budgets (the "594" in H6). A third definition; named, not in this slice.

### CSCS review

Nothing prescribed changes: the bands are the engine's existing ones, untouched (the gate holds
them against written numbers: 1,613-1,781 kcal, 156-188 g protein, 113-187 g carbs, 37-61 g fat for
Sam). What changes is what the screen SAYS about a day, which now matches what the engine did. One
figure means something different and it is deliberate: a lit protein number used to mean "at or
above target" and now means "inside the band a correct day keeps it in".

### Verified, and how

- `test:day-verdict` (new, 36 checks): 26 of 33 failed on the untouched tree. The header agrees
  with the engine on the day the engine actually serves, over 10,800 days (405 on target, 10,395
  off).
- `verify:day-verdict` (new, 22 checks, port 9653): four real Nutrition headers (on target, far
  under, over, and the page as it loads), each compared with what the engine's function says about
  the numbers on that screen, worked out in a child process from the app's module.
- Mutations: **18 tried, 18 caught** (15 logic, 3 in the browser).

### NOT verified

- No real screen in the harness has calories on target with one macro off, so "protein 26 g over"
  is proven by the logic gate and has not been read off a browser.
