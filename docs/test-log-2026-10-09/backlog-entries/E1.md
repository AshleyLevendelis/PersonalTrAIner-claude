### 1. L30 — a swapped-in exercise kept the old one's tempo; holds read "34-49s"

**Wrong.** Reproduced: the knee adaptation produced `Spanish Squat 3x30-45s
tempo=2-0-1` (a timed hold) and `Kettlebell Swing (Heavy) 4x10-12 ~24kg
tempo=2-0-1` (loaded and ballistic). `applyReplacement` copied the outgoing
slot and deleted the fields somebody had remembered. Tempo was the fourth to
leak. Building the slot from a list found three more nobody had reported:
`load_hold` and `rep_bump` (a bodyweight Low Box Step-Up carried "next weight
up is too big a jump" and "bought" from the walking lunge it replaced) and
`load_source` (a reported lift's provenance on a lift nobody reported).
Separately, a hold was moved week to week by the phase's REP shift, read as
seconds: 33-48s, 34-49s, 35-50s in one block, 27-42s in another.

**Changed.**
- `applyReplacement` BUILDS the slot from three sources (the incoming
  exercise, its own load, the slot's sets/reps/rest/effort). A field it does
  not name does not exist on the result.
- One rule, `isTempoEligible`, answers "may this lift carry a tempo" for
  generation and for replacement (it was inline in generation only).
- `describeExerciseTempo` is a second line of defence at the three places a
  tempo is printed (exercise line, expanded row chip, coach's plan context):
  never on a hold, a warm-up move or a swing. Plans saved before today still
  hold the stray cue until that slot is next edited.
- Holds step with `stepHoldSeconds`: week 1 base, +5s, +10s, deload back to
  base (30-45s, 35-50s, 40-55s, 30-45s). `shiftReps` snaps any seconds it is
  ever handed to a multiple of five, as a backstop.

**Decided as a CSCS coach.**
- *Holds step 5 seconds a week of the block; the phase's rep shift no longer
  moves a hold.* Basis: isometric holds are progressed in round blocks of time
  a person can count (5-10s is the usual step) up to about a minute, after
  which the variation is progressed rather than the clock; one second a week
  is below what anyone can execute or perceive. A phase's rep shift trades
  reps against load, and an unloaded hold has no load to trade, so a strength
  block was prescribing a shorter plank with nothing in exchange.
  1. Training effect: a real in-block overload (+10s, a third) where there was
     +2s of noise. 2. Takes away: the ±4s difference between phases, which
     nobody could perform. 3. Fundamentals: overload yes; the deload is now a
     real back-off (10s shorter than the week before). 4. Floors: the 10s
     minimum is untouched; week 3 tops out at 55s. 5. Scope: inside.
- *A warm-up (primer) hold stays at its base.* The app does not progress
  preparation.
- *A ballistic movement never carries an eccentric tempo cue.* Basis: ballistic
  and plyometric work is prescribed for intent and speed; slowing a swing's
  descent turns a hip snap into a front raise. `isBallisticMovement`
  (explosive groups, the primer swing, and any swing with a bell in the hand —
  not "Leg Swings", a mobility drill that shares the word).

**Verified.** `test:slot-replacement` extended (56 checks): the tester's two
rows by name; across all 210 live exercises, tempo survives exactly where
generation would give one; a field the type does not have cannot ride along;
the knee adaptation end to end. New `test:hold-seconds` (12 checks, 25 plans,
1,012 hold slots): every hold ends in 0 or 5, gets longer through a block, and
its deload is shorter. `test:tempo-prescription` §6 re-anchored (see below).
Mutations: 14 + 6 + 8, all caught; three were MISSED first (two redundant
"belt and braces" guards and the display guard's hold branch) and each got its
own direct check.

**Re-anchored on purpose.** `test:tempo-prescription` pinned
`describeTempo(ex.tempo)` and `<TempoChip tempo={ex.tempo}`; both sites moved
onto the display guard. The checks now hold "the screen shows the tempo
through the guard" plus the guard's own answers.

### 2. M32 — a replacement took the outgoing slot's reps and a weight that did not fit

**Wrong.** Reproduced exactly: with the flag cleared, banning Band Tricep
Kickback gave `Overhead Tricep Extension 3x16-19 ~22kg` in week 2. Two causes.
(a) A slot's `reps` is the block's range AFTER the outgoing lift's own levers
have worked on it, and the weight was priced for that range. (b) One fraction
priced every triceps isolation, written for a cable pushdown / EZ-bar
extension; a single dumbbell held overhead in two hands got the same number,
and only his stated 24kg limit kept it on the rack (~26kg at 8-12 without it).

**CORRECTION TO THE TRACER'S REPORT**, measured: the rep range was not the
main fault in this one case. A 14kg single-dumbbell lift is itself one of the
lifts whose reps walk up (a 2kg step is 14% of it), so generation would also
give it 16-19 in week 2. The WEIGHT was the defect. The class the tracer named
is real, though, and was measured the other way round the same day: a
bodyweight Low Box Step-Up took over a walking lunge's bought 11-13 beside
10-12 neighbours.

**Changed.**
- `repRangeForIncomingExercise(entry, profile, week)`: the rep range that
  exercise would be given in that week — the generator's own arithmetic for a
  lift it rotates into a slot mid-block (the incoming lift's bracket for its
  tier/style/goal/experience, the week's phase shift, and the week-in-block
  rep ramp only where reps are that lift's lever).
- `buildReplacementSlot` is the ONE way a slot is replaced (swap, ban,
  injury/kit adaptation, session rebuild): decide the work, price it for
  exactly that work, build the slot. The four callers each did those three
  steps by hand.
- A swap written into a DELOAD week is 70% of the lift's weight (generation's
  own rule for a slot with no week-3 number). It was written at full weight
  with the deload's eased-up reps: the hardest week of the block for that lift.
- A swap written into the CALIBRATION week starts light like every other
  unverified weight that week (it was at the full estimate, about twice the
  relative weight of everything around it).
- The week's tempo is taken from the WEEK, so the same step-up reads the same
  on both days it appears; a hold coming in takes that week's seconds and
  never more sets than the place it took.
- `SINGLE_IMPLEMENT_TRICEP_FACTOR = 0.65` on the reference, for a triceps
  isolation done with one dumbbell/kettlebell.

**Decided as a CSCS coach.**
- *A replacement is prescribed as itself.* Basis: a rep target is part of an
  exercise's dose, set from what limits THAT movement; a target earned by a
  different limiting factor prescribes neither exercise.
  1. Training effect: the replacement gets the stimulus generation would have
     given it, not the outgoing lift's. 2. Takes away: the frozen-weight rep
     bump the outgoing lift had earned (a like-for-like swap of a lift that had
     bought two reps starts the new lift without them). Deliberate: the bump is
     earned by a named lift across a block and generation gives a newly
     rotated-in lift none either. 3. Fundamentals: sets and rest stay the
     slot's, so volume, order and session time are unchanged; overload resumes
     from the lift's own baseline. 4. Floors: the experience rep floor and the
     hypertrophy main-lift floor are applied by the same call generation uses.
     5. Scope: inside.
- *One dumbbell overhead works at about two thirds of a pushdown.* Basis: the
  long head is at full stretch and the load is limited by shoulder position
  and by holding the bell, so it is joint- and grip-limited before it is
  strength-limited; 60-70% of a pushdown or EZ-bar extension for the same reps
  is the working range, and erring light is the right side on a first
  exposure. For the 82kg intermediate: about 17kg at 8-12 and 14kg at 15-18
  (was 26 and 22). A factor on the reference, not a new category, so the
  ceilings, load step and coherence groups do not move.
  **Measured, not assumed:** every prescription in the catalogue, before and
  after, across 2 sexes x 3 bodyweights x 4 experience levels x 3 dumbbell
  limits x 3 kettlebell limits x 2 tiers x 3 rep ranges x calibration on/off
  = 546,912 prescriptions. 2,280 changed, all of them Overhead Tricep
  Extension. Nothing else moved.

**Known approximations, stated.** On a deload week a weightless lift coming
in gets the phase's range; generation would cut two reps where cutting sets
bought nothing (it needs the loading week's set count, which one week does not
carry). And where generation's own weights are still climbing toward the
estimate, its deload can cut reps where a swapped-in lift cuts weight: 15 of
3,496 deload slots, never more than four reps apart.

**Verified.** New `test:replacement-prescription` (63 checks). The helper
against what generation itself printed, 48 seeded plans: weightless lifts
4,026 of 4,026, warm-ups 2,253 of 2,253, loaded lifts 2,890 of 3,008 (96%;
every difference is the one understood kind — generation has no baseline for
a lift it rotated in that week, the helper asks the lift's own estimate).
The tester's row; all four replacement paths (own range AND a weight priced
for that range, read against untouched neighbours on the same plan); deload
and calibration weeks; the factor and its two neighbours. 21 mutations, all
caught (one missed first: "factor spreads to every one-implement lift", which
needed an isolation neighbour to see it).

**Found, NOT changed (leads).**
- **Dumbbell Leg Curl is priced as a leg-curl machine**: a 48kg dumbbell held
  between the feet for an 82kg intermediate (and the "computed above the
  ceiling" warning fires for an advanced lifter). Same defect class as the
  triceps one; it came from the 10 Sep label fix, which made "per hand" into a
  total and so doubled the number. It needs its own factor (roughly 0.4) and
  its own measurement. The new gate pins that the triceps factor has NOT
  spread to it, by relation to the machine rather than by the 48.
- **`recomputeLoad`'s "conservative first prescription" for a new main lift
  has only ever been a sentence.** It passes `isFirstBlock: true`, which
  `prescribeLoad` accepts and reads nowhere. A swapped-in main lift gets the
  full estimate with "New lift — find your working weight" beside it. Noted in
  the code; changing the number is its own decision.
- **An ADDED exercise still copies a neighbour's rep range** (by Ashley's
  13 Sep design: an addition looks like the session it joins), so it can
  inherit a walked-up range the same way a replacement did.

### 3. H18 / H1 / H2 / H3 / L12 — the kit you said you own

**(a) "Week 2 now starts Romanian Deadlifts from your 30kg set" over a week 2

**The wording of the new clause is mine and is Ashley's to change.**

**(b) "What is your heaviest kettlebell?" asked by a one-dumbbell lift whose
limit was already known; Goblet Squats at ~32kg for a 24kg dumbbell owner.**
Reproduced: four one-dumbbell lifts asked the kettlebell question and their
own clamp ignored the answer. The clamp was re-routed by implement on 10 Sep
and the question was not. Now ONE function, `ceilingKindsFor`, is read by both,
and the prompt asks only when the clamp's own answer for that lift is "none".
*Decided as a CSCS coach:* a lift a dumbbell OR a kettlebell can do is capped
by the heaviest thing the person has told us they own that can do it — both
stated: the heavier; one stated: that one (this is the change; it used to read
the kettlebell answer only and fall back to a commercial rack); neither: no
cap. Basis: a prescription should not exceed what we know the person can
load; under-prescribing by a fixable margin is the safe side.
  1. Training effect: Goblet Squats, Suitcase Carry and Overhead Carry come
     down to a weight he can pick up. 2. Takes away: someone with a heavier
     kettlebell they never mentioned is capped at their dumbbells until they
     say so (Profile has the row); and they are no longer asked. 3.
     Fundamentals: unchanged. 4. Ceilings: this can now RAISE a cap for
     someone whose dumbbells are heavier than their kettlebell (16 → 18 in the
     measurement), never above anything they own or above the table. 5. Scope:
     inside. Measured on the same 546,912 prescriptions: 848 changed, across
     exactly those three lifts.

**(c) Chips "+2 · 32", "+4 · 34" after a logged 30 with a 24kg limit.** The
ladder moved into `nextSetRungsKg` and stops at a STATED limit (never a table
one). The "same" chip stays: what somebody actually lifted is not
