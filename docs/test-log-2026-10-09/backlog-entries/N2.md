## SLICE 1 — L28 + L8: pacing with a clock

### What was wrong (re-measured first)

Three rules said "behind"; one had a clock, and it was the machine's. Run against the untouched
rules, the new checks gave: at 07:00 with nothing eaten, "Protein is behind — 164g to go. Your lunch
has 59g of it, still to log."; at 08:30, "Water is behind — 2000ml to go."; on Home at 07:01 an amber
"no meals logged yet"; and at 21:53 on the day of sign-up, "about 1850ml behind on water". Home's
water tip read `new Date().getHours()` inside `loadDashboardData`, so under the harness's fixed day
it still moved with the wall clock (the gate printed "700ml behind" for every case, whatever time the
case said it was).

### What changed

- **One helper, `expectedByNow` (`src/lib/pace.ts`)**, on the APP's clock. Water is the existing
  08:00-22:00 pro-rata (Ashley's own example — unchanged arithmetic). Food is due meal by meal, each
  meal's SHARE of the day owed once its usual time has passed. Zero all day on the first day.
- **Nutrition's line** is quiet until something was due. "Behind" keeps its old threshold (a third
  and a bit outstanding), now asked of what was due BY NOW instead of the whole day — at the end of
  the day the two are the same rule. When somebody is keeping up but the meals left to log would not
  close the day, it says what is left in neutral words: "74g of protein to come today. Your dinner
  has 44g of it, still to log." Never "behind". Water gets no neutral line (the app plans food, not
  drinks; it would fire every morning).
- **Home's "no meals logged yet"** waits for breakfast's time to pass.
- **Home's water tip** is the same rule, on the app's clock, and quiet on the first day.
- **Nothing about pace on the day the account or the plan was made.**
- The dev clock gained an optional time of day (`?clock=HH:MM` in the harness) so a browser check can
  stand at 07:00 and 20:00 on the harness's one fixed day.

### Decisions, and whose

- **When the app may say "behind": decided unprompted, reversible** — the tracer's option A (only
  once the time has passed; quiet on day one). Ashley's to overrule.
- **The meal hours** (breakfast by 10:00, lunch by 14:00, snack by 17:00, dinner by 21:00): decided
  unprompted, reversible. Basis: the end of the usual British span for each meal plus about an hour,
  so somebody who eats a little late is not called behind at five past. The app does not ask anybody
  when they eat; if it ever does, that one table is what to replace.
- **The neutral sentence's wording** ("74g of protein to come today.") is a new app sentence. It
  follows the task's example; "74g" has no space to match the sentence that follows it on the same
  line ("has 44g of it"). Hers to change.

## SLICE 2 — L18: amounts somebody can measure

### What was wrong

One 0.795x resize gave "1.3 tsp", "0.8 tsp", "119g" and "239g": grams rounded to 1, spoons to one
decimal. Reproduced by the gate's first mutation (the old rounding put back prints 119).

### What changed

- **One rule, `kitchenRound`, in the scaler**: grams and ml to the nearest 5 from 20 up and to the
  nearest 1 below; spoons to the nearest quarter, never under a quarter; counted things whole, never
  fewer than one. Used by every path that resizes a dish — `scaleIngredients` (generation, the day
  search's resize, the refit, the leftover lunch), the slot move (which had its own copy of the old
  rounding) and the meal library's re-portion. Callers already re-cost from the returned lines, so
  the number on the card is the number costed.
- **On the meal card spoons read ¼ ½ ¾** ("1¼ tbsp olive oil"). The string handed to the edit sheet
  stays a decimal it can parse — two decimals now, or 1.25 would have gone back in as 1.3.
- **A follow-up found by this slice's own gates** (`785f7da6`): `test:leftovers` went red. Last
  night's dinner re-portioned as lunch lands on its protein floor to the gram, every caller asks for
  protein at or above target with no tolerance, and 252 g of chicken rounded to 250 g is 0.5 g short
  — the leftover lunch stopped being offered. `scaleToTarget` now holds the floor: when the exact
  resize reaches the protein asked for and the rounded one does not, the richest protein line that
  was rounded DOWN goes up one step instead. When the exact resize would not reach the floor either,
  nothing is nudged (the scaler does not start accepting dishes a plain resize does not reach).

### The measurement the task asked for

Meal library, 2,088 dish x target cases, through the real fit and the real verifier
(`measure:meal-library`'s own `servability`, counted exactly):

| | breakfast | lunch | dinner | snack | all |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 475/504 | 651/660 | 743/756 | 164/168 | **2,033 / 2,088 (97.37%)** |
| after | 477/504 | 651/660 | 742/756 | 162/168 | **2,032 / 2,088 (97.32%)** |

Four dishes lose one case each, three gain one. One snack, "Boiled egg with cottage cheese and
tomatoes", falls from 4 of 6 cases to 3 of 6 (under the measurement's 60% line): its egg cannot be
resized, and on a 170 kcal snack the cottage cheese no longer has whole grams to make up the
difference. **Accepted, not adjusted**: 27 other snacks serve, and the lost cases are calorie misses
on dishes with a whole egg in them.

A second, like-for-like measurement, because `test:meal-top-up` section 8 also went red: on the SAME
80 stored pools judged at four target drifts, the old rounding fits 2,547 dishes of 6,400 and the new
2,551; days on target 211 and 212 of 320. So the rule does not make dishes fit less often. That gate's
fixture draws its dishes by rejection with a seeded generator, so the scaler's rounding changes the
random stream behind every seed — seed 3 no longer drew three dinners that fit. **Re-anchored**: seed
7, found by the same scan, with the reason in the gate.

### Decision and CSCS review (it changes the size of what somebody is served)

Decided as a CSCS coach / nutrition generalist, population level.

1. *Effect, not the number:* the day's energy and protein are unchanged within rounding — 5 g on a
   240 g portion is 2%, inside the 7% a meal may miss its calories by and inside what a home scale
   and a "medium" potato already vary by. What changes is that the amounts can be weighed, which is
   what makes a plan followed.
2. *What it takes away:* whole-gram precision on small meals. Measured: one servable case in 2,088,
   and one snack below the 60% line. Between 20 and 50 g the step is a bigger share of a LINE (up to
   2.5 g in 20) but never more than 2.5 g of anything — at most 22 kcal of pure fat, under 5% of the
   smallest main meal the app plans. Under 20 g the foods are the dense ones (butter, honey, seeds),
   so they keep whole grams.
3. *Fundamentals:* energy balance and protein adequacy hold; the protein floor is now protected from
   rounding rather than left to it.
4. *A floor or ceiling quietly redefined?* No. The protein floor is the same number meaning the same
   thing. One behaviour beside it is new and deliberate: a dish that the exact resize puts ON the
   floor is no longer lost to rounding (it used to be a coin toss worth 0.3 g).
5. *Scope:* portion rounding only.

### Verified, and how

- `test:kitchen-amounts` (new, 42 checks): the test log's four numbers; the rule; the whole library
  through the real fit (1,900+ served dishes, every amount measurable, every card costed from its
  lines); the scaler across 100+ sizes; the slot move; no private rounding left in the meal files;
  the floor hold, including the case where it must NOT push.
- `verify:kitchen-amounts` (new, 10, port 9661): the real Nutrition tab, the app's own resize
  accepted, every amount read off the card; the spoon of oil reads "1¼ tbsp".
- Mutations: 20 tried, 20 caught (12 on the rule, 3 in the browser, 4 on the floor hold — one only
  after the check gained a case where the guard binds — and 1 on the harness-clock check).
- Gates the diff touches: 32 logic gates, all pass once the two above were dealt with; drivers
  `meal-food-edit`, `meal-refit`, `leftovers`, `meal-move`, `meal-top-up`, `day-verdict` pass.

### NOT verified

- **Real stored pools and live generation** — no database here. Meals already saved keep their
  amounts until they are next resized or regenerated.
- A cup is still costed and rounded as a count (N1's note stands).

### What was wrong

The heart had never saved a real meal since it shipped (19 Sep). It wrote the meal's protein, carbs
and fat with one decimal into `integer` columns; PostgREST rejects that (22P02) rather than rounding.
The heart is built not to move on a failed write, the handler discarded the answer, and nothing was
shown. Every check ran against a stand-in database that accepted 46.6.

### What changed

- The four numbers are rounded at the one write (`markFavourite`).
- A failed heart still does not look saved, and a line under the meal says so, from the shared
  phrasebook: "This favourite didn't save. Check your connection and give it another go."
- **The harness's stand-in database refuses a decimal in a whole-number column**, the way Postgres
  does, for every integer column the migrations declare (21 tables); a check re-derives the list
  from the migrations (`scripts/integer-columns.mjs`).

### What the stricter stand-in caught besides

- **Its own gap, at once**: hearting a meal twice adds one to `times_used`, which the real table
  defaults to 1 and the stand-in left empty (NaN). The stand-in now fills that default. No app change.
- **Nothing else on the nutrition side.** 27 browser drivers were run against the stricter stand-in
  (listed under "Gates"): all pass, so no other write those screens make puts a decimal in a
  whole-number column. **NOT run: the Exercise-tab drivers** (other lanes, and about eleven of them
  are hard-wired to another checkout), so `exercise_set_logs`, `workout_exercises`, `cardio_logs`
  and `workout_sessions` writers are not cleared by this. I did not trace any writer by hand.

### Verified

## Owner questions (plain language)

1. **When may the app say you're "behind"?** Built: only once the time for it has passed, quiet on
   your first day. The meal times it assumes are breakfast by 10, lunch by 2, a snack by 5 and dinner
   by 9. Keep that, never say "behind" at all, or let people set their own meal times later?
   Recommend keeping it.
2. **Oats and gluten.** Today plain oats pass the gluten-free filter and anything called "oat flour"
   is treated as plain flour and refused. Should oats (and oat flour) be allowed for gluten-free,
   refused, or allowed only when marked gluten-free? Recommend refused unless marked, which is how UK
