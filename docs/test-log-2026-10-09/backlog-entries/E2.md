### What changed

1. **Every track names the work without which its name is untrue**
   (`defining_patterns`, `defining_groups`) and a **named fallback**.
   `isTrackViable` requires one; the two hand-written label checks ("Squat &
   Carry" needs a squat, "Push & Press" an overhead press) became data. The
   fallback (`Upper Pull & Core` for a chest or shoulders day) is tried before
   the "richest track" search, and the day takes that name.
2. **Legs are out of "Shoulders & Abs" primary patterns.** One leg slot stays:
   filled last, written last, never a tier-1, never a movement that would
   outrank everything else on the day (so it cannot take the "Main lift"
   label), kept out of the set top-up, and not filled at all where two other
   days of the week already train legs. A **rear-delt slot** (admitted by
   substitution group — rows stay forbidden) and a **second core slot**, both
   "when the day has room"; the rear-delt slot is filled regardless where it is
   the only delt work the pool holds.
3. **Two family splits**, by named entry: `Neutral-Grip Dumbbell Press` →
   `neutral_grip_press`; the four pushdowns → `tricep_pushdown`, `Band Tricep
   Kickback` → `tricep_kickback`, overhead and lying extensions keep
   `tricep_extension`. New gate `test:movement-families` lists every split and
   merge in the override table with its reason.
4. **Thin-day borrowing**: a per-track `borrow` list (chest: rear delts, traps,
   trunk, trunk; shoulders: a row, traps, biceps; back: traps, trunk, trunk),
   read when a slot has nothing in the pool, again when the day is under its
   exercise count, and when the weekly balance pass removes an exercise —
   always before any time goes to optional mobility. Borrowed work is never a
   tier-1, never past two appearances a week, one per family, and never leads
   the day.
5. **The gap note is derived when the day is shown** (`src/lib/day-gap-note.ts`,
   from the day and the profile as they are now). The stamped
   `pattern_gap_note` field is gone from the type; plans stored before today
   still carry the old string and nothing reads it.
6. **One "day length, in parts" helper** (`dayLengthParts`, `formatDayLength`,
   `isSeparateSession` in `session-duration.ts`), read by the programme list,
   the Exercise tab's header and Home. The set count beside it is working sets.
   `FinishSection` decides what follows the lifting: "Finish" for a finisher
   and the optional close-out, "Also today · Separate session" for cardio the
   engine made its own session. L17: the header takes the length of the day
   that is OPEN; the warm-up badge names today-only drills apart from the
   plan's.
7. **The scorer's `day_label_mismatch` reads the defining patterns** through the
   same function the generator's viability check uses.

### Decided as a CSCS coach (basis with each)

- **A day named for a body part trains it or is a different day and says so.**
  Basis: specificity — a session's label is a claim about its training effect
  and the person plans their week by it.
- **What replaces shoulder work is upper-body pulling, the shoulder girdle and
  the trunk, not a third leg session 48 hours after leg day.** Basis: train
  around a flagged joint, bias pull over push, keep the scapular stabilisers
  and rear delts working (her 30 Aug and 24 Sep rulings).
- **A neutral-grip dumbbell press and a floor or barbell press may share a
  day; so may a pushdown, a kickback and an overhead extension.** Basis: the
  one-per-family test is "would a coach call these the same exercise".
  Different implement, grip and loaded range; three lengths for the long head.
  **Correction to the plan:** Landmine Press was never in `bench_press` — it is
  filed as an overhead press and already had its own family. Only two entries
  needed moving, not three.
- **The general fill stops at two triceps movements on a chest day**
  (`fill_caps`). Not in the plan; forced by measurement. The split handed the
  fill three triceps families where it had one, and an uninjured 60-90 minute
  chest day came out as four chest movements and four triceps: across the
  muscle-balance sweep (648 uninjured full-gym loading weeks) chest sets fell
  7,535 → 6,789. The split was for the day that had one triceps exercise, not
  a licence to trade pressing for arm work.
- **Where one press is all the pool holds, pulling is not trimmed or removed
  to match it** — in both weekly balance passes. Not in the plan; forced by
  measurement: the first version of borrowing put more pulling on Sam's week
  and the set-balance pass cut his back day's only row to two sets to "match"
  a four-set floor press. Her 24 Sep ruling chose "don't count it" *over
  cutting pulling to match*; the generator was still cutting. One function,
  `pullHeavyIsPrescribed`, now answers for the scorer and the generator.
- **A bodyweight, bodybuilding-style pool gets its trunk work back** (the style
  floor at bodyweight now reaches `core` as well as pushes and pulls). Not in
  the plan; forced by measurement: every core movement a bodyweight trainee can
  do is tagged functional/combat/hybrid, so that pool held none, which made
  both "Shoulders & Abs" and the named fallback unbuildable — the reason the
  plan's "worst row" (bodyweight + shoulder flag = legs on all four days)
  could not be fixed by the fallback alone. A plank needs no kit; a taste tag
  was starving a pattern the kit covers. It reaches no leg pattern, which is
  where the old duplicate cost was measured.
- **The refill loop refuses the same movement twice** (same family, same
  plane, an implement in common — `isGenuineDuplicate`, the scorer's existing
  line). It filtered once and pushed without re-checking, so one pass could
  admit a family several times: 94 of 288 seeded bodybuilding plans held a day
  with two or three exercises of one family. **Measured both ways before
  choosing:** strict one-per-family cost uninjured plans a tenth of their chest
  sets, because a long chest day had been getting Barbell Bench Press beside
  Incline Dumbbell Press through that loop — two angles, which a coach writes.
  So the line is the scorer's, not the family's. **Stated plainly: two or
  three VARIATIONS of one family are still admitted by that loop** (a
  dumbbell, a cable and a machine lateral raise on a 90-minute shoulders day;
  448 same-family pairs across 4,096 training days in the gate's sample). What
  is refused is the repeat: a Plank beside a Dead Bug, three bodyweight squats.
  **Corrected after the final run:** removing that line changes nothing on
  3,293 seeded plans, so it is inert as things stand — the repeats it names
  were removed by the other changes — and the "variations" it lets through
  include an improvised raise beside the real one, four times as often as
  before. See "What the final run found".
- **Borrowable work does not make a day viable.** First version counted it; a
  bodyweight, shoulder-flagged back day (one row, one curl) then "existed" on
  a borrowed plank and the week was three near-identical pulling days and one
  leg day. Without it that day falls to the old search and the week is pulling
  twice, legs twice.
- **Back & Biceps has no named fallback**, as the plan said — but for a
  measured reason, not "a row survives every flag": at bodyweight with a
  shoulder flag it does not survive as a day, and the right answer there is a
  second leg day.

### The five CSCS review questions, with numbers

1. **Training effect, not the number.** The session now does what its name
   says. Thin days (nine working sets or fewer padded with ten minutes or more
   of mobility) 596 → 0 of 2,304 plans; days not named for legs holding three
   or more leg compounds 1,142 → 0; Shoulders days with no shoulder work 256 →
   0. A flagged trainee with real kit goes from a week that barely pressed OR
   pulled (6.7 press sets, 6.7 pull) to one that does both (12.7 / 12.8 at
   full gym). Sam's chest day is 13 working sets where it was 7. Working sets
   per day, p10: 9 → 10 at 30-45, 9 → 12 at 45-60, 9 → 17 at 60-90, 10 → 18 at
   90+ — the thin tail is what moved; the medians are unchanged (13 / 16-17 /
   24 / 28).
2. **What it takes away, counted in the same run.** (a) Leg volume: median leg
   sets a week 36 → 27 uninjured and 36-39 → 23-26 flagged; bodyweight +
   flag 80 → 46. Leg FREQUENCY is untouched: a leg compound on two days a week
   for every one of the 2,304 plans (it was two, or three to four at
   bodyweight). The second exposure is a single leg movement at two sets,
   last, on all 2,112 Shoulders days in the slice. It is not always "light":
   on 136 of them it is a loaded bilateral lift a coach would not call an
   accessory (Romanian Deadlifts 82, Hack Squat 21, Leg Press 16, Kettlebell
   Swing 9, Smith Machine Squat 8, plus Good Mornings 63 if you count them) —
   never a tier-1, always two sets, but worth your eye. (b) Uninjured
   chest sets −6.6% (7,535 → 7,035 across 648 uninjured full-gym weeks),
   because a short chest day is now three chest movements and two triceps;
   triceps volume on that day doubles. (c) 69 more uninjured full-gym plans
   (130 → 199 of 384) have a day whose required work is under the shortest
   session asked for — the long Shoulders day, which legs used to pad. Its
   remainder is optional mobility and the label says so. (d) Sam's week has
   Rear Delt Flyes on three days (9 sets) and a row and shrugs on two: it is
   the only rear-delt and the only row family his kit and flag leave. That
   repetition is real and is the dumbbell-catalogue gap the plan holds for the
   kit-list decision.
   (e) **Found by the final run, not by the grid table above:** the quality
   score falls about 0.04 like for like (11.522 → 11.481 on a 768-plan
   sample), all of it improvised kit beside better kit — a band triceps
   movement as the second on most chest days with equipment, and a backpack
   or band raise beside the dumbbell one on 479 of 1,728 Shoulders days (was
   101). Numbers and the fix I tried and withdrew are under "What the final
   run found".
3. **Fundamentals.** Push, pull, squat and hinge all still appear weekly
   wherever the pool holds them: plans with no pull 0 → 0, no legs 0 → 0, no
   press 192 → 192 (bodyweight + shoulder flag, where no press exists at all).
   Recovery improves — no squat-pattern main lift 48 hours after leg day.
   Progressive overload is untouched (no load, rep or phase rule changed).
