# Reading generated plans as a coach (18 Nov 2026)

His brief: "The exercise tab and plans making sure they are world class." Method: generate 60 to 160 seeded people's
16-week plans, print the cards, and read them the way a CSCS coach reads a programme before signing it. Each finding
below was counted before anything was changed. Every decision here is a training decision and so is mine under his
18 Sep delegation; the basis is written beside each. Nothing here is rehab, diagnosis or clinical.

## What was found, and what is done about it

### 1. Loaded lifts prescribed past 20 reps (BUILT, `test:rep-ceiling`)
3,380 of 21,000 loaded slots asked for more than 20 reps, 889 for more than 25 ("Overhead Tricep Extension 3x24-29").
A loaded lift's range now ends its block at 20 or fewer; weightless lifts are not capped. Basis: NSCA muscular
endurance is 12 or more reps and coaches write 12 to 20; past that a loaded set is limited by discomfort.

### 2. Side lifts given 3 to 5 reps
In strength and power blocks every lift's range drops by 3 or 4 reps, down to the experience floor (3 for advanced,
4 for intermediate). Measured: about 470 of 43,524 slots are a lift that is NOT the day's main lift with a range
starting under 6: "Walking Lunges 2x3-5 @38 kg a hand", "Dumbbell Lateral Lunge 2x3-5", "Renegade Row 2x3-5",
"Single-Arm Lat Pulldown 3x3-5", "Push-Ups 4x4-6 Bodyweight", "Band Lat Pulldown 3x4-6", "Ab Wheel Rollout 2x3-5".

**Decision: only the day's main lift goes under 6 reps.** Every other lift's range starts at 6 or higher.
Basis: NSCA assigns the heavy strength loads (6 reps or fewer) to core exercises only and limits assistance
exercises to loads no heavier than an 8RM; 6 reps with two in reserve is about an 8RM. A one-leg, one-arm or
balance-limited lift at a 3 to 5 rep load is limited by control, not by the muscle, and a weightless lift at 4 reps is
simply an easy set. Main lifts (the day's tier-1 lift) are untouched and still go to 3-5.
- What it takes away: those lifts get lighter (weight follows reps). The strength block's heavy work stays where it
  belongs, on the main lift. Push Press is the one ballistic barbell lift outside warm-ups and would run 6-8 in a
  power block instead of 3-5; a coach signs either for a general trainee, so it gets no exemption.
- One function gives the floor to generation, the swap helper and the base assignment.

### 3. Warm-up drills moved by the block
A warm-up drill starts at 5 reps and was then pushed around by the phase like a working lift: 3 arm circles in a
strength block for an advanced lifter, 9 in a conditioning block, 8 in every block for a beginner, and one more each
week on some goals. Measured: about 1,150 of 43,524 slots are a warm-up drill at 5 reps or fewer.

**Decision: movement prep is not periodised.** A drill's dose depends on what the drill is and is the same in every
block and week: explosive prep (jumps, slams, plyo push-ups, swings) 5 reps; activation and mobility drills 10 reps.
Basis: a warm-up's job is to raise, activate, mobilise and potentiate (RAMP); activation and mobility work is done
for about 8 to 12 controlled reps, potentiation for a few fast ones with full quality. The code already said "the app
does not progress preparation" and held timed drills still; rep drills were the gap.
- Ankle Alphabet becomes a timed drill (30-45 s a side), like the balance hold beside it: a "rep" of an alphabet was
  never countable.
- These are warm-up doses for light, unloaded or band work. Nothing about which drill is given for which joint
  changes.

### 4. Two weights no coach would write
Read off the heaviest weight each exercise was given across 160 people:
- **Dumbbell Leg Curl up to 48 kg** (one dumbbell held between the feet; median 16 kg). It is priced as a machine
  leg curl. It now takes a third of that price: about 6 kg at the median and 22 kg for an advanced 95 kg lifter
  (0.4 was tried first and still gave him 26).
- **Dumbbell Tricep Kickback up to 20 kg a hand**, the same as a lying extension. A kickback is hardest where the
  arm is weakest; it now takes half the extension's price (10 kg at the top).
Both use the existing "lighter variant" rule, which can only lower a number.
- Named, not changed: a **Trap Bar Carry** is priced at the farmer's-walk figure for ONE hand as its total (50 kg at
  most where the same person carries 48 kg a hand). It errs light, the safe side; raising a weight needs its own
  trace.

### 5. Two deadlift days and no squat
A full-body day's main lift may be a squat or a deadlift, picked on its own. Measured on 80 plans' second weeks: 7
had two full-body days both built on a deadlift variant (Sumo Deadlift then Deadlifts), and 4 of those had no squat
main lift anywhere in the week (a 2-day beginner: deadlift both days).

**Decision: a week's lower-body main lifts alternate.** When a day may be built on either, it takes the pattern the
rest of the week holds less of (days already built, and later days whose main lift is fixed). Basis: both patterns
trained weekly is the first thing a coach checks on a programme; two heavy hinge sessions with no squat main lift is
a coverage gap and loads the lower back twice. Where an injury or the kit leaves only one pattern, the day keeps it.

## CSCS review (the five questions)
1. Training effect: heavy work stays on the main lift; side lifts and warm-ups sit in the ranges that train what they
   are for; squat and hinge are both trained as main lifts.
2. What it takes away: weight on side lifts in strength and power blocks; nothing else. Counted in the same runs
   (plan quality mean, repeated week-pairs, sessions over length).
3. Fundamentals: pattern coverage improves (5); overload unchanged (reps, weight or both still climb); recovery
   helped (5, 2); specificity kept (main lift untouched).
4. Existing floors: the experience rep floor keeps its meaning for the main lift; the new floor is a separate, named
   number for everything else. Checked that the hypertrophy and conditioning main-lift floors still bind.
5. Scope: programming only.
