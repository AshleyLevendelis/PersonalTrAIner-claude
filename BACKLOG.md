Two compounding mechanisms, both confirmed by direct trace and by re-running the audit with bodyweight/gender varied (see below): (1) categorize() in load-prescription.ts checks generic substrings before specific ones — `n.includes('squat')` and `n.includes('deadlift')` fire before the later `n.includes('bulgarian')/'split squat'` and `n.includes('romanian')` checks, so "Bulgarian Split Squats" gets the FULL barbell-squat standard instead of the scaled-down single_leg_dumbbell category (0.4x), and "Romanian Deadlifts" gets the FULL barbell-deadlift standard (the highest of any lift) instead of hinge_accessory (0.55x) — while still loaded on a light dumbbell/kettlebell. (2) `lastUnverifiedLoadingWeekKg` deliberately never resets at block boundaries (by design, per its own doc comment — "the next block's week 1 must step from the last real loading week") — for a profile with no logged/known weight, this ratchets upward for the entire 16-week mesocycle with no ceiling of its own, relying entirely on prescribeLoad's own clamp against a fresh estimate. Combined, a miscategorized exercise's much-higher fresh-estimate ceiling lets the ratchet climb far past what the implement can hold. Re-ran the audit with the new bodyweight/gender sweep (see next item): the ceiling clamp fired 7933 times in one run — Romanian Deadlifts (2354) and Bulgarian Split Squats (911) dominate, confirming this isn't the 62kg profile's edge case, it's pervasive. Calf raises/shrugs/rows/carries (smaller counts) look like mechanism (2) alone, since those names are correctly categorized. The ceiling clamp caught every one — nothing unsafe reached a displayed plan — but this needs a real fix, not just the safety net.
