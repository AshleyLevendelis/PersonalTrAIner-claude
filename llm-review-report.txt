2. **Day titles don't match the content, and the load numbers are template defaults, not coaching.** "Pull & Hinge" Tuesday is a deadlift followed by 7 sets of Dumbbell Bench Press (week 1) / Incline DB Press (week 3) — that's horizontal pressing the day after 14 sets of horizontal pressing. Loads are worse: week 3 has Barbell Curls at 7x15-18 @ 20kg for a beginner who is bench pressing 25kg. Nobody curls 80% of their bench. Lateral raises jump from 5kg cable to 10kg dumbbells per hand for 15-18 reps at RPE 6-7. Bulgarian split squats at 12kg per hand x 11-15 x 7 sets in week 3 of a beginner's first block will wreck them. Squat, bench, deadlift and row are all "~20kg" in week 1 — that's the empty bar for all four, which for a deadlift means the plates aren't even at pulling height. This is a fill-in-the-blank template, not calibration.

No hamstring curl, no RDL, no calf, no hip thrust. Quads/hams/glutes get roughly 9 hard sets a week while triceps get 14 in one session. Meanwhile the exercises rotate between week 1 and week 3 for no stated reason — push-ups→DB bench, walking lunges→Bulgarian split squats, cable rows→DB rows, lat pulldown→assisted pull-ups. A beginner needs to repeat the same lift for 4-6 weeks to build the motor pattern and see the load go up. You've made progression untrackable and stapled a harder unilateral variation into week 3 with no ramp.

TOP 3 STRENGTHS:

Most auto-generated programs either skip the deload or "deload" by only removing 10% of the load. This one actually reduces the stimulus.

VERDICT: Yes. A beginner handed 14 sets of push-ups and dips on day one, a 90-minute session sold as 45-60, and a week-3 curl prescription heavier than their bench press will either get injured, quit in the first fortnight, or figure out the numbers are fake — and the 2-sets-of-squats-vs-7-sets-of-lunges structure tells any second opinion immediately that no coach looked at this before it shipped.
